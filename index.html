Privacy Policy for DirectServe

Effective Date: November 17, 2026

DirectServe ("DirectServe: Cast & File Share") is made by an independent developer who publishes apps under the name GhostGram Labs. GhostGram Labs is not a company. This policy explains how DirectServe handles information.

1. Summary

  • DirectServe works on your own Wi-Fi network or your phone's hotspot. It does not need the internet.
  • There is no account, no cloud upload, no analytics, no ads and no tracking.
  • The developer does not collect, receive or store any of your files or personal data.

2. How the App Works

When you tap Start sharing, DirectServe runs a small web server on your phone. Devices on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot open its address (or scan its QR code) in a web browser to watch, browse, download or upload the files you chose to share. Everything travels directly between your phone and those devices over your local network. Nothing passes through any server run by the developer or anyone else.

Sharing stops when you tap Stop. While it is on, a notification shows that sharing is active.

3. Files and Media You Share

DirectServe reads only the videos, photos, music, documents and folders you choose to share, so that devices you connect can view or download them. If a browser can't play a video as it is, DirectServe converts it on your phone. Converted copies are kept in the app's private storage on your phone, with a size limit: the oldest are removed automatically, and all are removed when you clear the app's cache or uninstall.

Files that other devices upload to your phone are saved on your phone only after you approve each transfer.

4. Screen Mirroring and Sound

Mirror screen starts only when you open it, tap Start and accept Android's screen-capture prompt. Your screen is streamed live, over your local network only, to the browsers you connect. To stream it, DirectServe may buffer the stream in a temporary file in the app's private cache on your phone. That file is never sent anywhere else, other apps can't read it, and it is removed when you clear the app's cache or uninstall. Android shows a capture indicator for the whole session.

Share sound is off by default. If you turn it on, DirectServe captures the sound that other apps play (such as a video or game), so it can be heard on the other device. It does not record your voice: the microphone input is switched off. Android requires the microphone permission for this kind of sound capture, which is why DirectServe asks for it. Some apps block their sound from being captured.

5. Text and Links

Text and links you send between your phone and another device with Send text stay on your phone and in the browser pages you connect. They are not sent anywhere else.

6. Connected Devices and Logs

To show who is connected and let you rename or block devices, DirectServe keeps basic local network details (such as a device's local IP address and name) and a history of what was shared on your phone. These stay on your phone and are never sent to the developer. You can clear the history in the app.

7. Security

Anyone on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot who has the address can open your share, so share only on networks you trust. You can require a PIN, approve each upload, see connected devices, block a device, and stop sharing at any time.

8. Smart TVs and Media Players (DLNA)

If you turn on DLNA in Advanced settings, DirectServe announces your phone on your local network so smart TVs, TV boxes, VLC and Kodi can find it. This announcement stays on your local network.

9. Permissions

  • Network and Wi-Fi state, multicast: to run the local sharing server, show its address and let TVs find it with DLNA.
  • Photos, videos, music and files: to list and share the media and files you choose.
  • All files access (optional): only if you turn it on, to share documents and whole folders outside your photo and music library. DirectServe works without it.
  • Foreground service: keeps sharing or screen mirroring running while you use other apps, with a notification and a Stop button.
  • Screen capture: for Mirror screen, only after you accept Android's prompt.
  • Microphone (record audio): required by Android for capturing other apps' sound when you turn on Share sound. Your voice is not recorded.
  • Notifications: to show that sharing or mirroring is on and to let you stop it.
  • Keep awake and battery optimisation exemption: so a share doesn't stop when the screen turns off.
  • Internet: required by Android for any network connection, including your local network. DirectServe does not send your data to the internet.

10. Data Sharing and Third Parties

No data is shared with or sold to third parties. DirectServe contains no analytics, advertising or tracking libraries. It uses open-source libraries (such as Ktor, AndroidX Media3 and WebRTC) that run on your phone.

11. Children

DirectServe is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
